STUDENT GUIDE

A little preparation.
A better learning day.

Practical guidance for your first online lesson, supporting a child, and building a routine that works across time zones.

01 / GETTING READY

Your first online lesson.

Choose a quiet, comfortable space where you can hear clearly and keep your reading material close. A phone, tablet, or computer with stable internet and working audio is a good starting point.

Confirm the lesson platform and any materials with the academy beforehand. Test your microphone and speaker, charge your device, and join a few minutes early if your lesson arrangements allow.

A small helpful habit

Keep a notebook for pronunciation reminders, practice targets, and questions to revisit.

A moment of reflection

Pause with a short Quranic passage, its meaning, and a gentle prompt for your learning day.

02 / LITTLE AND OFTEN

Make practice manageable.

A short session with a clear focus is easier to repeat than an ambitious plan you cannot sustain. Agree on appropriate practice with your teacher, and adapt it to your level.

  1. Revisit. Begin with a familiar exercise or passage from your previous lesson.
  2. Focus. Work slowly on one letter, rule, or correction at a time.
  3. Reflect. Note what felt clear and what you want to ask next time.

For memorization, keep older revision in the routine. Avoid adding new material simply to meet an arbitrary target.

03 / SUPPORT AT HOME

Encourage without rushing.

Help your child prepare their device and materials, and keep a parent or guardian available for lesson arrangements. Ask what they learned, celebrate effort, and let questions be part of the process.

For younger learners, discuss suitable lesson length, supervision, breaks, and communication with the academy. Adults should manage enrollment and contact details for children.

04 / LEARNING WITHOUT BORDERS

Be clear about your time zone.

Include your country, city or time zone, and two or three preferred time windows in your inquiry. For example: “London, weekday evenings after 6 pm local time.”

Some countries change clocks during the year. Confirm the actual lesson time in both your local zone and the teacher’s zone, and check again when daylight saving changes. Availability is confirmed individually; a preferred time is not a booking.

Before agreeing on a schedule

Ask about the weekly frequency, rescheduling arrangements, missed lessons, holidays, and how changes will be communicated.
